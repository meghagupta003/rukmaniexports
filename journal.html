Why the World's Rarest Pearls Are Almost Gone

Long before cultured pearls existed, the finest natural pearls in the world came from the oyster beds of the Persian Gulf — pearls so prized that Basra became one of history's great pearl-trading centers, giving these stones their name. Decades of environmental change and industrial development have left those natural beds largely depleted. Most pearls sold today, even fine ones, are cultured. A genuine natural Basra Pearl is now one of the rarest things a collector can hold — and one of the specialties our family has traded longest. Every Basra Pearl we offer is independently certified as natural, not cultured, by a recognized gemological laboratory.
